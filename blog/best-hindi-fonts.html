20 Best Hindi Fonts of 2026

Ranked by category — serif, sans-serif, display, and calligraphy. With use-case recommendations and free Google Fonts download links.

10 min read·Published March 10, 2026·Updated August 30, 2026·By Akshay

Choosing the right Hindi font is one of the most impactful design decisions you can make for any project involving Devanagari text. The right typeface communicates personality, context, and professionalism before a single word is read.

We have tested and ranked 20 of the best Hindi fonts available today — all free, all open-source, all sourced from Google Fonts.

Category 1 — Display Fonts

Display fonts are designed to be seen at large sizes. They work for headlines, posters, YouTube thumbnails, and social media graphics where impact matters more than extended readability.

1. Modak — Best for: festive & playful content
नमस्ते भारत

Modak's inflated, rounded letterforms burst with energy. Named after the Indian sweet, it radiates joy. Perfect for children's content, food brands, and festive greetings. Download →

2. Baloo 2 — Best for: versatile display use
उत्सव आनंद

Five weights, rounded and warm. Baloo 2 is the most versatile display font for Devanagari — it transitions from bold headline to approachable body text better than almost any other display face. Download →

3. Teko — Best for: tech & sports
भारत देश

Narrow, tall, geometric. Teko commands attention in tight spaces and looks excellent at very large sizes. Ideal for sports graphics, news tickers, and tech brand content. Download →

4. Rozha One — Best for: editorial headlines
रचनात्मक लेखन

A strong, confident serif display face with high contrast between thick and thin strokes. Rozha One looks excellent in editorial layouts, magazine covers, and anywhere a heavyweight serif is needed. Download →

Category 2 — Sans-Serif Fonts

Sans-serif fonts are the backbone of digital Devanagari — optimised for screen readability, available in multiple weights, and legible at all sizes from mobile captions to desktop UI.

5. Noto Sans Devanagari — Best for: everything digital
डिजिटल भारत

The gold standard. Nine weights, comprehensive glyph coverage, flawless rendering at all sizes. When in doubt, use Noto Sans Devanagari — it works for UI, body text, headlines, and everything in between. Download →

6. Hind — Best for: web body text & UI
मेरा भारत महान

Humanist, open, and purpose-built for the web. Hind was designed specifically for Indian language digital publishing and renders beautifully in body text at 14–18px. Pairs perfectly with Rajdhani for headings. Download →

Category 3 — Serif Fonts

Serif Devanagari fonts carry authority and tradition. They are the first choice for books, newspapers, academic publications, formal documents, and cultural projects that need a classical feel.

7. Martel — Best for: editorial & news
समाचार पत्र

A robust humanist serif drawing from Indian newspaper typography traditions. Seven weights, excellent for editorial layouts and news sites. One of the most complete Devanagari serif families available. Download →

Category 4 — Calligraphy Fonts

Calligraphy and handwriting fonts bring warmth and personality to Devanagari text. Essential for wedding stationery, greeting cards, motivational content, and any design that needs a human touch.

8. Kalam — Best for: handwritten & personal content
हस्तलिखित पत्र

The most popular Devanagari handwriting font. Kalam genuinely looks handwritten without being messy — a difficult balance achieved remarkably well. Perfect for quotes, diary-style content, and casual social media. Download →

9. Tillana — Best for: cultural & artistic content
संगीत नृत्य कला

Named after a rhythmic Indian classical composition, Tillana has flowing, dance-like letterforms. Refined enough for formal invitations, expressive enough for art and cultural branding. Download →

How to Choose the Right Hindi Font for Your Project

Choosing the wrong font is one of the most common mistakes in Hindi design. A font that looks stunning on a poster can be completely wrong for a website. A font that reads beautifully in a book might look amateurish on a social media graphic. Here is a practical framework for making the right choice every time.

Match Font Personality to Content Tone

Every Hindi font has a personality — and that personality should match the tone of your content. Modak communicates joy and celebration. Teko communicates authority and modernity. Kalam communicates warmth and informality. Before choosing any font, ask: what emotion should this text create in the reader? Then pick the font whose personality matches that emotion.

Match Font Weight to Display Size

At large sizes (72pt+), use lighter weights. At small sizes (below 16pt), use heavier weights. A common mistake: using Regular weight Devanagari at 10pt, where the shirorekha (the horizontal line at the top of Hindi letters) becomes too thin to read clearly. For body text under 14pt, always use Medium or SemiBold, never Regular or Light.

Test Conjunct Consonants in Your Actual Text

Before committing to a font, type your actual headline or paragraph and check how the conjunct consonants look. Words with complex conjuncts like "ज्ञान", "क्षमा", "त्रिशूल" will reveal a font's quality immediately. If conjuncts look cramped or misaligned, the font is not the right choice regardless of how good the individual letters look.

Hindi Fonts by Use Case — Complete Reference

For YouTube Thumbnails

Best choices: Teko SemiBold, Modak, Baloo 2 ExtraBold. The key requirement for thumbnails is that text remains readable at 120×68 pixels (the size thumbnails appear in mobile search results). Use fonts with thick strokes and simple letterforms. Avoid calligraphy fonts or thin weights — they become illegible at thumbnail size. Always add a 3–4px black text stroke for visibility against photo backgrounds.

For Instagram and Social Media Graphics

Best choices: Baloo 2 Bold (warm, versatile), Modak (festive content), Kalam Bold (personal quotes), Teko (bold news-style posts). For Instagram bio text that needs to copy paste directly, use our font generator — stylised Unicode text works in bios without any font installation on the viewer's device.

For Wedding Invitations and Cards

Best choices: Amita Bold for the couple's names, Laila Regular for venue and date details, Tillana for decorative elements. Wedding cards need fonts that photograph well (for WhatsApp sharing), print cleanly on card stock, and carry an appropriate emotional register — celebratory but not childish, elegant but not cold.

For MS Word and Official Documents

Best choices: Noto Sans Devanagari Regular or Medium (most comprehensive character coverage), Hind Regular (clean screen rendering), Mangal (pre-installed on Windows, required for some government portals). Avoid display fonts — Modak, Baloo, Teko — for document body text. They are designed for large sizes, not extended reading.

For Logo and Brand Identity

Best choices: Rajdhani (modern, geometric, multiple weights), Hind SemiBold (clean professional), Rozha One (traditional premium), Teko (tech and sports brands). Logo fonts need to work at very small sizes (business card, favicon) and very large sizes (signage, banner). Test at both extremes before finalising.

For Photoshop and Digital Design

Any font works in Photoshop once installed. The requirement specific to Photoshop: enable the World-Ready Composer (Type → Language Options → Enable World-Ready Composer) before typing Hindi. Without this, Devanagari letters don't connect properly regardless of which font you use. This is the most common Hindi Photoshop problem and has nothing to do with the font itself.

Common Hindi Font Mistakes — And How to Avoid Them

Using Kruti Dev for New Projects

Kruti Dev is a legacy ASCII encoding font — it cannot be indexed by search engines, cannot be copy-pasted correctly across devices, and does not work on modern apps or websites. Many older designers still use it out of habit. For any new project, use a Unicode Devanagari font instead. If you have old Kruti Dev documents that need converting, use our Kruti Dev to Unicode converter.

Choosing Fonts by How They Look on a Specimen Page

A font specimen page always shows the font at its best — the right size, the right background, carefully chosen sample text. A font that looks beautiful on Google Fonts may look wrong for your specific use case. Always type your actual text in the font before deciding.

Using Too Many Fonts in One Design

Most professional Hindi designs use two fonts maximum — one for display/headlines and one for body text. Three or more fonts in a single design almost always looks cluttered and amateurish. If you use two weights of the same font family (e.g. Hind Bold for headings and Hind Regular for body), it counts as one font decision — always a safe approach.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Hindi font is best for beginners?

Noto Sans Devanagari is the best starting point for anyone new to Hindi typography. It handles every character and conjunct correctly, works at all sizes, and is appropriate for almost any context. Once you're comfortable, explore Hind for screen-focused work and Baloo 2 for display and social media work.

Can I use these Hindi fonts for commercial projects?

Yes. All fonts listed here are from Google Fonts under OFL or Apache 2.0 license — both allow commercial use with no per-project fees. You can use them in client work, products, apps, and anything you sell commercially.

Which Hindi font is pre-installed on Windows?

Windows includes Mangal, Aparajita, Kokila, and Utsaah as pre-installed Devanagari fonts. Mangal is the most commonly required for government portals and official documents. For better-looking fonts, download from Google Fonts and install — it takes under a minute.
